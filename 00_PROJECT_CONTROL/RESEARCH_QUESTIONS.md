# Research Questions

## RQ-001: Textual Transmission

What textual form of the Manava-Dharmashastra was available to European translators between 1794 and 1886, and how does that textual form relate to the manuscript tradition reconstructed by Patrick Olivelle in 2004?

### Subquestions

RQ-001-A
What Sanskrit text did William Jones use?

RQ-001-B
What role did Kulluka Bhatta play in Jones's textual base?

RQ-001-C
What Sanskrit sources did Georg Buhler use?

RQ-001-D
Which traditional commentaries did Buhler consult?

RQ-001-E
What manuscript evidence was available to Buhler?

RQ-001-F
What textual principles did Buhler apply?

RQ-001-G
Where does the Jones/Buhler textual tradition differ from Olivelle's critical text?

RQ-001-H
Where do Jones, Buhler and Olivelle differ on disputed passages?

RQ-001-I
Can each difference be attributed to:

1. manuscript variation
2. recension
3. scribal transmission
4. editorial reconstruction
5. commentary
6. translation
7. interpretive choice
8. unsupported alteration

No difference is to be classified as deliberate falsification without independent evidence.

## RQ-002: Kulluka

How does the Kulluka recension relate to other surviving Manusmriti manuscript traditions?

## RQ-003: Interpolation

Which passages can be demonstrated to be secondary through manuscript, recension, linguistic, metrical, chronological or commentary evidence?

## RQ-004: Translation

Which apparent differences between Sanskrit Manusmriti and later English representations are caused by translation or interpretation rather than differences in the underlying Sanskrit text?

## RQ-005: Historical Practice

To what extent can Manusmriti prescriptions be demonstrated to correspond to actual historical social and legal practice?

## RQ-006: Modern Reception

How did colonial and modern political, social and ideological movements select, reinterpret or contest particular Manusmriti passages?

## Research principle

The research must distinguish:

TEXT
from
RECENSION
from
COMMENTARY
from
TRANSLATION
from
INTERPRETATION
from
HISTORICAL PRACTICE
from
MODERN RECEPTION.
