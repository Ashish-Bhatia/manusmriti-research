# Research Method

## Central question

What form of the Manusmriti is recoverable from the evidence, how did it develop and transmit, and how did later recension, commentary, editing, translation, colonial law, and modern ideology affect its interpretation?

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Manuscripts and manuscript catalogues
2. Critical editions and apparatus criticus
3. Primary Sanskrit texts
4. Early and medieval commentaries
5. Inscriptions and archaeology
6. Contemporary or near-contemporary literature
7. Peer-reviewed scholarship and academic presses
8. Scholarly translations containing Sanskrit
9. Colonial legal and administrative sources
10. Popular or ideological material

## Rules

- Never treat a translation or modern quotation as equivalent to Sanskrit evidence.
- Never call a passage an interpolation merely because it is controversial.
- Separate interpolation, recension difference, scribal error, editorial reconstruction, mistranslation, and later interpretation.
- Separate composition date, redaction date, manuscript date, commentary date, printing date, and translation date.
- A normative prescription is not evidence of actual social practice without independent corroboration.
- When sources disagree, record both sides and explain the evidentiary basis.
- Do not infer deliberate falsification without evidence.
- Do not fabricate quotations, manuscript readings, dates, citations, or scholarly consensus.

## Interpolation test

For every alleged interpolation examine:

1. Manuscript distribution
2. Recensional distribution
3. Earlier citations
4. Commentary evidence
5. Language
6. Vocabulary
7. Metre
8. Concepts and doctrine
9. Chronological compatibility
10. Parallel passages

## Translation test

For disputed passages compare:

- Sanskrit
- grammatical construction
- literal meaning
- Jones
- Buhler
- modern academic translations
- traditional commentaries
- alternative interpretations

Distinguish mistranslation from legitimate ambiguity. Do not infer intentional distortion without evidence.

## Historical-practice test

Do not infer actual Indian social practice from Manusmriti prescriptions alone. Compare inscriptions, legal records, regional literature, Buddhist and Jain evidence, archaeology, foreign accounts, and other normative texts.

## Research principle

The objective is neither to defend nor attack Manusmriti.

The objective is to establish what the evidence supports.
