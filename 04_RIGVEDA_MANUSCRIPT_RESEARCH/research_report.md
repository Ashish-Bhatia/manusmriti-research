# Research Report: The 1361 Benares Rigveda Manuscript

Date: 2026-09-03

## Executive finding

A fourteenth-century Rigveda manuscript is securely documented in specialist scholarship. Michael Witzel identifies a Benares Sanskrit University manuscript covering RV 8.6.25–10.191 and dated 1361 CE. His footnote gives Samvat 1418 and Cat. No. 40 XIV, and records his brief personal sighting of the manuscript in early 1973 while it was displayed in a glass case.

The modern IGNCA Saraswati Bhawan Library catalogue separately identifies A229/56093 as a Rigveda manuscript. The evidence does not yet establish that A229/56093 is Witzel's 1361 manuscript.

## Identity fingerprint

`RV 8.6.25–10.191`

`1361 CE`

`Vikrama Samvat 1418`

`Benares Sanskrit University`

`Cat. No. 40 XIV`

`Seen by Witzel in early 1973`

This combination is sufficiently specific to function as the manuscript's research fingerprint.

## Historical catalogue

The Benares Sanskrit College manuscript catalogue was published as a supplement to *The Pandit* from 1869–1875. The bibliographical survey identifies the catalogue as *Rājakīya-Vārāṇasī-vidyāmandira-Sarasvatibhavana-varti-pustakānām Sūcipatram*.

The 1869 first section is the critical archival target:

- Vol. 3, No. 35A
- pp. I–IV: Rigveda, approximately 100 manuscripts
- pp. V–XII: Rigveda and Yajurveda, approximately 250 manuscripts

The historical catalogue therefore offers a manageable route to locating the entry corresponding to `40 XIV`.

## Modern IGNCA record

IGNCA's Saraswati Bhawan Library catalogue lists repository A229 records in a Vedic cluster. Record 56093 is classified under Vedas > Rigveda > Vedic literature > Hinduism-Sacred books.

Adjacent A229 records include Vedic literature, Gṛhyaprayoga, Śrautaprayoga and Nirukta. This establishes the relevance of A229 to the Saraswati Bhawan Vedic collection, but adjacency is not evidence of manuscript identity.

## Chronology

The date relationship should be recorded carefully. Samvat 1418 falls in the 1361–62 CE period. Witzel gives the manuscript date as 1361 CE. Without the exact colophon date, the repository should not silently convert Samvat 1418 into a single Gregorian day or assume the entire Vikrama year equals calendar year 1361.

## Historical significance

The manuscript is an unusually early surviving Rigveda witness. Witzel's discussion places it among the earliest Vedic manuscripts known in the literature at the time, while also noting older Vedic or para-Vedic material from the Kathmandu Valley. It should therefore be described as an early Rigveda manuscript, not as the oldest Vedic manuscript overall.

## Provenance hypothesis

The working provenance chain is:

`1361 manuscript`
→ `Samvat 1418`
→ `RV 8.6.25–10.191`
→ `Benares Sanskrit University`
→ `Cat. No. 40 XIV`
→ `Benares Sanskrit College/Saraswati Bhawan catalogue tradition`
→ `modern Saraswati Bhawan Library records`
→ `IGNCA catalogue`

Only the final object-level crosswalk remains unresolved.

## What is proven vs inferred

### Proven

- The manuscript existed.
- It was a Rigveda manuscript.
- Its reported range was RV 8.6.25–10.191.
- It was dated 1361 CE / Samvat 1418.
- It was associated with Benares Sanskrit University.
- Its historical catalogue reference was Cat. No. 40 XIV.
- Witzel saw it in early 1973.
- A modern IGNCA A229/56093 record is classified as Rigveda.

### Not proven

- A229/56093 is Cat. No. 40 XIV.
- A229/56093 is dated Samvat 1418.
- A229/56093 covers RV 8.6.25–10.191.
- The current physical manuscript behind A229/56093 is the object Witzel saw in 1973.
